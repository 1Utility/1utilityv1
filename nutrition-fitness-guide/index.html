TDEE Explained: Your Daily Energy Expenditure

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total calories your body burns in a day. Understanding it is foundational to any fitness goal—whether you want to lose fat, build muscle, or maintain.

Your TDEE consists of four components:

Components of TDEE

  1. BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate): Calories burned at rest (60–75% of TDEE). This is your baseline—energy used for breathing, heartbeat, cellular function.
  2. NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis): Calories burned from daily movement: walking, working, fidgeting (15–20% of TDEE). Often underestimated.
  3. TEF (Thermic Effect of Food): Calories burned digesting food (~10% of TDEE). Protein requires more energy to digest than carbs or fat.
  4. EAT (Exercise Activity Thermogenesis): Calories burned during formal exercise (5–10% of TDEE).
Example: A 75kg, sedentary person might have:

BMR: 1,700 cal/day
NEAT: 400 cal/day (desk job, minimal movement)
TEF: 170 cal/day
EAT: 0 cal/day (no exercise)
TDEE = 2,270 calories/day

Same person who exercises 4 days/week could have TDEE of 2,800 cal/day (+530 from exercise alone).

How to Calculate Your TDEE: Two Methods

Method 1: Harris-Benedict Formula (Fastest)

Men: BMR = 88.362 + (13.397 × weight kg) + (4.799 × height cm) − (5.677 × age)

Women: BMR = 447.593 + (9.247 × weight kg) + (3.098 × height cm) − (4.330 × age)

Then multiply by activity factor:

  • Sedentary (no exercise): BMR × 1.2
  • Light activity (1–3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
  • Moderate activity (3–5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
  • Very active (6–7 days/week): BMR × 1.725
  • Extremely active (2x/day training): BMR × 1.9

Method 2: Track & Measure (Most Accurate)

  1. Track food intake for 7 days (use app like MyFitnessPal)
  2. Weigh yourself daily for 7 days, calculate average
  3. If weight is stable → your average daily intake ≈ your TDEE
  4. If weight increased by 0.5kg → add 250 cal/day to your eating estimate (0.5kg × 7,700 cal per kg ÷ 14 days)

Macronutrients 101: Carbs, Protein, Fat

Macros are the three nutrients your body needs in large quantities. Each serves a distinct purpose.

Macro Calories per gram Key Functions Typical % of Diet
Protein 4 cal/g Muscle growth, recovery, immune function, satiety 25-35%
Carbohydrates 4 cal/g Energy (especially for brain & exercise), fiber, nutrients 45-55%
Fat 9 cal/g Hormone production, nutrient absorption, satiety, brain function 20-35%

Balanced Macro Split Example

For a 2,500 calorie day:

  • Protein: 30% = 750 cal = 187g
  • Carbs: 45% = 1,125 cal = 281g
  • Fat: 25% = 625 cal = 69g

Key insight: These ratios are guidelines, not strict rules. The most important thing is hitting your protein target and controlling total calories. Carb/fat ratio is flexible based on preference.

How Much Protein Do You Actually Need?

This is the most misunderstood aspect of nutrition. Here's what the science says:

Protein Recommendations

Goal Protein per kg Protein per lb Example (75kg person)
Sedentary / Maintenance 0.8 g/kg 0.36 g/lb 60g/day
Fat loss (dieting) 1.6-2.2 g/kg 0.7-1.0 g/lb 120-165g/day
Muscle building (surplus) 1.6-2.0 g/kg 0.7-0.9 g/lb 120-150g/day
Very intense training 2.0-2.2 g/kg 0.9-1.0 g/lb 150-165g/day

Practical Rule of Thumb

Aim for 1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight if you're training. This covers muscle building, fat loss, and recovery.

For a 75kg person training 4+ days/week: 120–150g protein/day

Vegetarian & Vegan Protein Challenge

Plant-based proteins are less bioavailable than animal proteins. Vegetarians/vegans should aim for the higher end: 2.0–2.2g per kg.

Why? Plant proteins have:

  • Lower digestibility (70–90% vs 95%+ for animal sources)
  • Incomplete amino acid profiles (lacking some essential amino acids)
  • Higher fiber (filling, but less energy for muscle building)

Calorie Deficit Science: Why It's the Foundation of Fat Loss

Want to lose fat? This is non-negotiable: You must consume fewer calories than you burn. No food is "too bad" if you're in a deficit. No food is "magic" if you're not.

The Numbers

  • 1 kg of body fat ≈ 7,700 calories
  • Deficit of 500 cal/day = 0.5kg fat loss/week ≈ 2kg/month
  • Deficit of 750 cal/day = 0.75kg fat loss/week ≈ 3kg/month

Note: You can't just eat less protein and expect fat loss. If calories drop but protein drops too, you'll lose muscle along with fat.

Optimal Deficit Strategy

Goal: Lose 8kg Fat in 4 Months

Required deficit: 8kg × 7,700 cal = 61,600 total. Divided over 120 days = 513 cal/day deficit.

Strategy:

  • TDEE = 2,500 cal/day
  • Target = 2,000 cal/day (500 deficit)
  • Protein = 150g/day (non-negotiable for muscle preservation)
  • Exercise = 4 days/week resistance training (critical for maintaining muscle)

Expected outcome: 8kg fat loss, muscle maintained or slightly built (new trainee effect).

Common Deficit Mistakes

  • Too aggressive (1,500+ deficit): Leads to muscle loss, hormonal disruption, extreme hunger. Result: Look worse at lower weight.
  • Too low protein in deficit: Body preferentially loses muscle, not fat. You lose weight but look no different.
  • Ignoring NEAT: Sedentary people create accidental deficit just by moving more. Walk, take stairs, stand while working.

Debunking Common Nutrition Myths

Myth: "Carbs are bad for weight loss."

Reality: Weight loss is about total calories, not macros. A study comparing low-carb vs high-carb diets with same calories showed equal fat loss. Carbs don't cause fat storage; calorie surplus does. Choose carbs you enjoy.

Myth: "Eating fat makes you fat."

Reality: Fat doesn't make you fat. Excessive calories do. Fat is calorie-dense (9 cal/g) so easy to overeat, but healthy fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado) are essential for hormones and health. Include them.

Myth: "You need supplements to build muscle."

Reality: Muscle is built from food (protein) + training + sleep. Supplements are optional convenience. Whole foods work just fine. Creatine and whey are the only evidence-backed supplements for muscle building.

Myth: "Vegetarian/vegan diets can't build muscle."

Reality: Plant-based diets can build muscle if protein target is met (higher intake needed ~2.0g per kg). Many elite vegan athletes (Serena Williams, Lewis Hamilton) prove this false.

FAQ

Q: How often should I eat?

A: Meal frequency doesn't matter. Total daily calories and protein do. Some people thrive on 2 meals/day; others prefer 5. Choose what lets you hit your targets comfortably.

Q: Should I count calories or intuitive eat?

A: Count calories for the first 4–8 weeks to learn portion sizes and build intuition. Then you can often eyeball it. But if you're not hitting targets, go back to counting.

Q: Is it okay to have a "cheat day"?

A: You don't need cheat days if you include foods you enjoy daily (within your macros). But if eating 2,000 cal pizza once weekly keeps you sane and adherent, it's worth it. Adherence > perfection.
