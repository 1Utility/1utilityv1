Exact pixels vs. percentage

Use exact pixel dimensions when you need a specific size — a profile picture at 400×400, a banner at a fixed width for a website. Use percentage scaling when you just want to shrink or enlarge an image proportionally without calculating exact numbers — 50% halves both dimensions, 200% doubles them.

Locking the aspect ratio

With aspect ratio locked, changing the width automatically recalculates the height to match the image's original proportions, and vice versa — this prevents accidentally stretching or squashing the image. Unlock it only if you specifically want to force a different width-to-height ratio.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will resizing reduce image quality?
Enlarging an image beyond its original size will make it look softer or blurrier, since there's no new detail to add. Shrinking an image generally looks sharp — you're just discarding pixels, not inventing missing ones.
What format is the output?
The resized image keeps the same format as your original upload — a JPG stays a JPG, a PNG stays a PNG.
Can I resize to a larger size than the original?
Yes, though enlarging beyond the original resolution will look softer, as there's no additional real detail to add.
Is my image uploaded anywhere?
No — resizing happens entirely in your browser using the Canvas API. Nothing is sent to a server.
