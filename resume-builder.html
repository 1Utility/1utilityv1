Getting Past the ATS

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) is software recruiters use to scan, store, and rank resumes before a human ever opens them. Most large companies use one, and a resume that an ATS can't parse correctly often never reaches a recruiter — regardless of how qualified the candidate is.

What ATS parsers struggle with: text inside images or graphics, content in tables used purely for layout, headers/footers (some parsers skip them entirely), unusual fonts, and icon-only section labels with no text equivalent.

What works reliably: standard section headings ("Experience", "Education", "Skills"), reverse-chronological dates, plain bullet points, and keywords pulled directly from the job description's required skills and responsibilities.

Every template here is built from real text in a standard reading order — no text-in-image tricks, no layout tables. Sidebar-style templates (Tech, Marketing, Sales, HR) look more visually distinct but place contact/skills information in a side column; for roles where you suspect a stricter older ATS will be used, the single-column templates (Finance, Consulting, Government, Legal) are the safest choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this resume builder actually free?
Yes — no payment, no sign-up, no watermark on the downloaded PDF. The site is supported by display advertising.
Where is my resume data stored?
Only in your browser's local storage on this device. Nothing is uploaded to a server — close the tab and reopen it later and your draft will still be there, but it won't follow you to another device or browser.
How do I download a PDF?
Click "Download as PDF" on the preview step — this opens your browser's print dialog. Choose "Save as PDF" as the destination/printer instead of an actual printer.
Can I switch templates after entering my details?
Yes — your information is stored separately from the template choice, so switching templates on the preview step re-renders the same data in the new layout instantly.
Do any templates claim to be preferred by specific companies?
No. Templates are organized by role/industry convention (e.g. the conservative single-column format common in finance recruiting), not by claimed endorsement from any named employer — no company certifies or endorses resume templates.
