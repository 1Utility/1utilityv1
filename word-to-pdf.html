How it works

Your .docx file is parsed directly in the browser to extract its text, headings, formatting, tables, and images, then rendered as clean HTML. From there, clicking "Download as PDF" opens your browser's native print dialog — choose "Save as PDF" as the destination instead of a physical printer, and you get a real, searchable, text-based PDF rather than a flattened image of your document.

What carries over, and what doesn't

Headings, bold and italic text, paragraphs, bullet/numbered lists, basic tables, and embedded images all convert cleanly. What doesn't carry over: exact fonts and custom colors, precise spacing, multi-column layouts, headers/footers, footnotes, and tracked changes/comments. For a standard report, letter, or document built from normal paragraphs and headings, the conversion is clean. For a heavily designed flyer or brochure-style document, expect a simplified result.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my formatting be preserved exactly?
Headings, bold/italic text, paragraphs, basic tables, and images carry over. Precise fonts, custom colors, multi-column layouts, headers/footers, and footnotes are not preserved — the underlying conversion library intentionally focuses on document structure over exact visual styling.
Is my document uploaded anywhere?
No. The .docx file is read and converted entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing is sent to a server.
Why does it use my browser's print dialog instead of downloading directly?
This produces a real, searchable, text-based PDF rather than a PDF that's just a picture of your document — the same approach used by the print-to-PDF feature built into every modern browser.
Does this work with .doc files (the older Word format)?
No — only the modern .docx format (Word 2007 and later) is supported. Older .doc files would need to be re-saved as .docx first, which Word itself can do via "Save As".
What happens to images in my document?
Embedded images are preserved and appear in the output PDF in roughly the same position within the text flow as in the original document.
